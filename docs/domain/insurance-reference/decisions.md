# Insurance Reference Decisions

## Ambiguity Resolutions

- "Which public examples upgrade to V2" could mean only the default customer-facing package or every source-controlled public example. Resolved: both **Insurance Customer Service Agent** and **Institution Insurance Specialist Agent** public example packages upgrade to **React Enterprise QA Template V2** so `examples/` does not carry mixed V1/V2 template semantics.
- "V2 Intent Resolution disclosure in public examples" could mean showing intent details to every caller or preserving per-audience disclosure. Resolved: the customer-facing **Insurance Customer Service Agent** keeps Intent Resolution and review facts internal, while the staff-facing **Institution Insurance Specialist Agent** may expose governance details through allowed operator/internal projections.
- "Customer response" could mean the full internal run response or a customer-safe shape. Resolved: customer-facing surfaces use **Customer-Safe Response Projection** and internal operator/developer surfaces use governed audit projections.
- "Customer projection" could mean rewriting the run result without audit linkage. Resolved: projection is allowed for customer safety only when the exact customer-visible **Customer Response Snapshot** is linked to the governed run.
- "Customer-visible tool source" could mean exposing the tool name or internal run reference. Resolved: customer-facing responses use **Customer-Safe Source Label** values, and internal run artifacts map those labels to specific **Authorized Tool Result** records.
- "Product term explanation" could mean generic evidence-backed interpretation or a personalized coverage decision. Resolved: V1 supports **Insurance Product Term Interpretation** only when grounded in accepted evidence or authorized tool results and not framed as a guarantee or legal advice.
- "Insurance service process" could mean explaining how the process works or executing a service action. Resolved: V1 supports **Insurance Service Process Guidance** for evidence-backed process explanation and safe next-step options, not transactional action, SLA commitment, or claim outcome decision.
- "Anonymous insurance question" could mean generic product/process explanation or a personalized service request. Resolved: anonymous sessions may ask generic term and process questions, but **Personalized Insurance Service Request** handling requires authentication.
- "Am I covered" could mean explaining relevant coverage terms or making a personalized coverage/payment decision. Resolved: V1 may explain terms, process, and authorized status facts, but **Personalized Coverage Or Payment Decision** is out of autonomous scope and maps to **Payment Or Coverage Guarantee Request** when a conclusion is requested.
- "Improve approval odds" could mean a document checklist or **Outcome Optimization Advice**. Resolved: V1 may provide evidence-backed preparation steps and required materials, but must not predict, optimize, or imply improved claim, coverage, eligibility, approval, or payment outcomes.
- "Unsafe process wording" could mean refusing the whole request or safely reframing it. Resolved: V1 uses **Safe Process Guidance Reframe** when possible, and refuses or hands off only when the user insists on prediction, guarantee, personalized decision, or rule evasion.
- "Friendly wording" could mean harmless conversational text or unsupported business advice. Resolved: **Safe Conversational Text** may be unaudited for evidence, but every **Customer-Facing Business Claim** requires **Accepted Evidence** or an **Authorized Tool Result**.
- "Multilingual customer service" could mean Chinese/English support or arbitrary language coverage. Resolved: V1 uses **Customer Response Language Policy** for Chinese and English only.
- "Translation" could mean a customer-safe projection or a new fact-generation layer. Resolved: V1 allows **Evidence-Bound Translation** only for claims already supported by **Accepted Evidence** or **Authorized Tool Result** values, with source references anchored to the original source.
- "V1 acceptance" could mean isolated demo questions or complete customer journeys. Resolved: V1 uses a **Customer Journey Acceptance Suite** covering anonymous, authenticated, refusal, clarification, internal handoff, retrieval failure, and bilingual paths.
- "Journey acceptance test" could mean a compatibility smoke test or a hard release blocker. Resolved: keep always-on smoke assertions for current customer-safe behavior, and use V1 release-gate assertions to expose unmet release requirements until strict validation is enabled.
- "Customer feedback" could mean an observation signal, run artifact fact, memory source, or online learning input. Resolved: V1 uses **Customer Feedback Signal** for observation only, keeps it linked to conversation turn and governed run, and does not create **Case Memory**, train, or update behavior.
- "Customer-visible answer history" could mean recomputing from trace, using the complete visible message as memory, or storing what the customer actually saw. Resolved: V1 stores the complete text as a **Customer Response Snapshot** linked to the governed run; **Case Memory** may only use bounded, trace-safe facts or summaries derived from that snapshot linkage.
- "V1 release" could mean private enterprise pilot or public internet production. Resolved: V1 is a **Private Pilot Customer Service Bot**; public-scale operations are future platform work.
- "Streaming" could mean customer-safe stage progress or raw model tokens. Resolved: V1 exposes **Customer Run Progress State** only; token streaming is future verified streaming work.
- "Customer chat API" could mean reusing the internal Chat API or adding a customer-safe endpoint. Resolved: V1 uses **Customer Run API** for customer-facing runs and keeps internal Chat API responses for operator/developer surfaces.
- "Customer-visible Agent not found" could mean unknown Agent id only or unavailable for customer audience. Resolved: customer-facing discovery and errors expose only **Customer-Facing Published Agents**, and **Customer Run API** rejects non-customer-facing Agents at conversation creation.
- "Customer chat Agent selection" could mean selecting both an Agent and customer identity. Resolved: Agent selection and customer session mode are independent; customer identity is supplied through **Customer Authorization Context** during customer conversation creation.
- "Enterprise QA intelligent customer service" could mean the whole product or the first Agent built with it. Resolved: use **Enterprise QA Reference Agent** for the first Agent and keep Proof Agent as the framework.
- "Insurance customer service Agent" could mean the existing insurance QA example or the V1 customer-facing Agent. Resolved: use **Insurance Customer Service Agent** for the V1 Published Agent and keep the existing insurance QA example as a baseline package.
- "Intelligent customer service" could mean direct customer-facing automation or staff assistance. Resolved: V1 delivery is **Autonomous Customer Service Mode**; **Assisted Service Mode** is a separate staff-assistance mode.
- "Chat frontend" could mean a customer-facing chatbot or a staff workbench. Resolved: V1 chat is a **Customer Service Chat Frontend**; **Assisted QA Chat Frontend** is the operator-facing surface.
- "Customer-facing Agent" could mean an Agent id allowlist, workflow template, purpose text, or contract capability. Resolved: a **Customer-Facing Published Agent** is a Published Agent whose Agent Contract declares a top-level customer section.
- "Customer channel" could mean Web chat, messaging apps, email, mobile SDK, or contact-center integration. Resolved: V1 ships **Customer Service Web Chat**; other channels are future adapters.
- "Customer intake" could mean text questions or uploaded customer documents. Resolved: V1 uses **Text-Only Customer Intake**; attachment analysis is future work.
- "Customer-safe text" could mean no Harness run is needed for prompts or refusals. Resolved: Safe Conversational Text may not require Accepted Evidence, but every **Customer Run API** turn still needs a governed run and non-empty RunStore `run_id`.
- "Customer chat session" could mean anonymous policy browsing or authenticated account service. Resolved: use **Anonymous Customer Session** for generic-only access and **Authenticated Customer Session** for customer-specific service.
- "Customer authorization check" could mean API preflight or Harness-governed policy context. Resolved: Customer Run API may resolve mock identity, but **Customer Authorization Context** must be admitted into the Harness run so PolicyEngine, Tool Gateway, trace, receipt, and review paths can explain customer-specific access.
- "Customer resource selection" could mean choosing a default resource, presenting safe disambiguation options, or deterministically resolving one. Resolved: V1 requires **Customer-Owned Resource Resolution** to identify exactly one owned resource before a customer-specific read; otherwise it returns a **Customer Resource Disambiguation Prompt**.
- "Only one matching resource" could mean still asking for an id or proceeding with the unique resource. Resolved: when the **Owned Resource Handle Index** has exactly one matching customer-owned resource, V1 may proceed to single-resource lookup proposal without a disambiguation prompt.
- "Resolution audit" could mean customer-visible explanation or internal run basis. Resolved: **Customer Resource Resolution Basis** is an internal artifact fact recorded in Trace, Governance Receipt, and RunStore; customers see only safe handles or source labels.
- "Partial resource identifier" could mean a safe customer-facing handle or a raw backend identifier. Resolved: V1 disambiguation prompts may show **Customer-Safe Resource Handle** values such as fixture-provided ids, last-four identifiers, dates, or type labels, but not status, amounts, coverage, internal customer ids, raw payload fields, or authorization details.
- "Resource handle source" could mean authenticated owned-resource metadata or a hidden batch lookup. Resolved: **Customer-Safe Resource Handle** values come from an **Owned Resource Handle Index** supplied by auth context, mock personas, or a future identity adapter, not from running status lookup tools in bulk.
- "Resource count" could mean disambiguation metadata or a bulk account summary. Resolved: V1 may show matching resource counts from the **Owned Resource Handle Index** only to explain selection, not as status, amount, coverage, or eligibility information.
- "Disambiguation option state" could mean authorization, memory, or conversation context. Resolved: use **Customer Disambiguation Option Mapping** inside **Controlled Conversation Context**, linked to the originating run and **Customer Response Snapshot**, not **Customer Authorization Context** or **Case Memory**.
- "Disambiguation lifetime" could mean reusable conversation memory or a one-step clarification aid. Resolved: **Customer Disambiguation Option Mapping** is valid only for the current clarification sequence and expires on successful lookup, replacement prompt, identity change, conversation reset, Agent change, or timeout.
- "Expired disambiguation" could mean recovering stale option mappings from conversation history or asking again. Resolved: expired or absent **Customer Disambiguation Option Mapping** forces a new **Clarification Request**; old transcripts, snapshots, and **Case Memory** cannot restore it.
- "Customer authentication" could mean proving the Harness authorization boundary or integrating a production identity provider. Resolved: V1 uses **Mock Authenticated Customer Session** and reserves production OAuth/OIDC/IAM for **Customer Identity Adapter** work.
- "Mock customer" could mean a single demo identity or multiple authorization fixtures. Resolved: V1 uses at least two **Mock Customer Persona** fixtures and tests **Cross-Customer Access Attempt** behavior.
- "Cross-customer access" could mean a normal refusal or an internal security signal. Resolved: V1 returns customer-safe refusal wording and records an internal **Customer Escalation Handoff** for every **Cross-Customer Access Attempt**.
- "Customer service automation" could mean read-only answers or business-state changes. Resolved: V1 is **Read-Only Customer Service**; state-changing work is a **Transactional Customer Action** and is out of scope.
- "Institution insurance specialist workflow" could mean a short-term-insurance-only topology or a reusable staff-facing insurance workflow. Resolved: use **Institution Insurance Specialist** as the generic assisted-service user, and constrain short-term insurance deployments through **Insurance Business Line Scope** rather than a Harness-coded workflow fork.
- "Institution business system access" could mean read-only query support or direct business operations. Resolved: V1 **Read-Only Institution Assistance** may use **Institution Business Read Tool** bindings for reports, policies, claims, customers, agents, and configured business-line records; state-changing work is a **Transactional Insurance Operation** and is out of scope.
- "Insurance source truth" could mean knowledge documents, report data, business-system records, or the model's synthesized judgment. Resolved: use **Insurance Source Authority Order**; records answer current state, documents answer rules, reports answer metrics, and unresolved conflicts remain explicit instead of being reconciled by the model.
- "Conflicting insurance rules" could mean choosing the most semantically relevant passage, trusting the newest-looking file, or applying approved business authority. Resolved: determine **Insurance Rule Applicability** first, then apply **Insurance Rule Precedence Order** before advisory synthesis; a model may explain the resolved basis but cannot create, override, or guess rule authority.
- "Rule metadata from PDF and Excel" could mean treating extracted fields as authority, reconciling two competing drafts, or using both surfaces in one governed review. Resolved: parsers and models produce an immutable **Insurance Rule Metadata Parser Proposal** that initializes a **Current Insurance Rule Metadata Review Draft**; Dashboard and Workbook edit that same draft, and only business-confirmed **Approved Insurance Rule Metadata** may determine applicability or precedence and enter a published Knowledge version.
- "Automatically review many repeated metadata proposals" could mean letting AI approve business authority, showing every identical Rule Unit as separate work, or generating one compact suggestion for human confirmation. Resolved: private Hybrid parsing may produce one Profile-shaped **AI Metadata Review Suggestion** as a Document Default plus exact Rule Unit proposals; matching units inherit that default, only genuine differences or incomplete values remain Overrides, and the Dashboard requires explicit reasoned human confirmation against the exact Review identity. Missing or malformed output fails closed and AI never gains approval authority.
- "Require a metadata workbook before publication" could mean making XLSX import the only way to create reviews or treating it as an optional bulk-edit surface over the governed review lifecycle. Resolved: every completed Hybrid build automatically creates an **Insurance Rule Metadata Review** set; required default and exception reviews must be approved before publication, while workbook export/import is optional and direct Dashboard review remains available. A deployment policy may separately require workbook provenance without making XLSX the default product workflow.
- "Generate an editable metadata workbook" could mean publishing a blank template, constructing internal identities in the browser, or exporting an exact server-owned review snapshot. Resolved: only an **Insurance Rule Metadata Workbook Export** produced by Proof Agent may enter the matching import path; it binds an export identity to the exact Source, document revision, canonical anchor set, and review generation. External automation first requests that export and then returns the edited file; hidden cells and workbook protection are usability aids, never the validation boundary.
- "Organize the metadata workbook" could mean retaining one opaque technical table, separating every concept into unrelated files, or presenting the governed review hierarchy in one source-bound workbook. Resolved: `insurance-rule-metadata.v2` has the fixed allowlisted sheets `Instructions`, `Document Defaults`, `Rule Unit Overrides`, read-only `Reference Values`, and hidden `_Manifest`. The default sheet contains one editable default row; the override sheet contains every canonical Rule Unit with locked review context, inheritance mode, review reason and safe source preview, while filters initially emphasize required or overridden rows. Reference Values provides labels and spreadsheet validation from the bound Insurance Metadata Profile Revision. Workbook import proposes draft changes only and never performs business approval.
- "Enter insurance metadata codes" could mean accepting any nonblank string or validating against exact business vocabularies. Resolved: one **Insurance Metadata Profile Revision** defines allowed authority values, taxonomy revision, precedence policy and tiers, ordering constraints, and governed date rules. Dashboard uses labeled selection, Workbook uses read-only `Reference Values` plus dropdowns, `_Manifest` binds the Profile revision, and import rejects values outside that exact revision; adding a code requires a new Profile revision.
- "Own insurance metadata vocabularies" could mean copying code lists into every Source, applying one mutable deployment-global list, or binding reusable immutable governance. Resolved: **Insurance Metadata Profile** is a shared Knowledge Hub asset with immutable published revisions; each Source Draft binds one exact **Insurance Metadata Profile Revision**. Profile changes do not mutate existing Sources or publications, and an explicit Source binding upgrade shows impacted defaults and overrides, invalidates metadata readiness and prepared validation, and requires renewed review before publication.
- "Upgrade a Source to a new Metadata Profile Revision" could mean clearing every field, automatically trusting code mappings, or previewing exact semantic impact. Resolved: **Metadata Profile Upgrade Preview** automatically carries only stable IDs whose constraints are unchanged, treats label-only changes as ready for renewed approval, presents explicit replacement IDs as unapproved suggestions, clears removed unmapped values, and blocks incompatible precedence, taxonomy, or date constraints for input. Preview is non-mutating; atomic Apply binds exact old/new Profile digests, Source revision, and review generations, creates new current Review revisions, invalidates prepared publication, and preserves all historical approvals.
- "Import an offline workbook after concurrent review edits" could mean rejecting every stale review generation, overwriting newer server values, or reconciling from an exact base. Resolved: template, environment, Source, document-revision, or anchor-set mismatch makes a **Structurally Stale Metadata Workbook** and rejects the whole operation. When only review content changed, **Metadata Workbook Import Preview** performs a field-level three-way comparison of export base, current server review, and returned workbook; unresolved conflicts block application, and confirmed conflict-free changes apply atomically rather than partially or silently.
- "Retain metadata workbook content" could mean deleting every file immediately, retaining every export forever, or separating temporary editing from applied audit provenance. Resolved: **Metadata Workbook Export Validity Window** defaults to thirty days and closes on expiry, structural staleness, or successful application; an unapplied Preview uses the same temporary retention and leaves only bounded digest-level audit after purge. **Applied Metadata Workbook Import Record** retains original, normalized, merge, and decision artifacts under Knowledge configuration audit retention. Export, download, upload, preview, conflict resolution, and apply use Proof Agent APIs with `knowledge_source.edit` and audit, never direct S3 URLs or content-bearing audit projections.
- "Migrate metadata workbook V1" could mean rewriting published authority, retaining V1/V2 runtime dual writes, or cutting current Drafts over explicitly. Resolved: published Knowledge versions remain byte- and authority-stable; **Legacy Metadata Workbook History** stays read-only for audit; **Metadata Review V2 Migration** requires a published Profile binding, materializes a new current Review Set from the completed build without PDF re-upload, carries eligible legacy values as attributed draft input, and requires renewed approval. V1 imports fail with `metadata_workbook_template_obsolete` and a V2 export action after cutover; migration is explicit, dry-run-capable, repeatable, and never runs at application startup.
- "Deploy Metadata Review V2" could mean a dark dual-version rollout or one incompatible maintenance-window cutover. Resolved: stop Source writes and Workers, verify backup and migration preconditions, run the explicit schema/data migration, deploy the V2 Dashboard/API/Worker image together, verify the full Source flow, then reopen writes. No V1 endpoint, parser, UI, dual read, dual write, runtime fallback, or feature flag ships in the new release. Legacy facts are migrated into read-only audit history rather than read through V1 authority. Failure before reopening restores the pre-cutover database/artifact snapshot and old image; after the first V2 mutation only forward repair is valid.
- "Provide the first Insurance Metadata Profile" could mean inferring vocabulary from uploaded PDFs, silently using a demo Profile in production, or separating local workflow enablement from production authority. Resolved: designated local environments check in and publish `proofagent-insurance-reference.v1` as a **Reference-Only Insurance Metadata Profile** and may bind it to the local `ks_insurance` fixture during direct migration without re-uploading its PDF. The Profile contains only explicitly declared reference codes and is visibly marked `reference_only`. Production never auto-seeds or auto-binds it: the **Production Insurance Metadata Profile Precondition** requires an authorized published non-reference Profile selection before migration, and later replacement of a reference binding uses Metadata Profile Upgrade Preview plus renewed approval.
- "Decide when the direct V2 cutover is safe to open for writes" could mean relying on deployment health, checking only the happy path, or requiring bounded cross-surface evidence. Resolved: the cutover acceptance gate blocks reopening until migration dry-runs are repeatable, Workbook V2 round trips pass in Excel and LibreOffice, hostile-package and configured limit tests pass, concurrent Dashboard/Workbook edits plus Profile upgrade and Prepare expiry preserve authority, `ks_insurance` completes build-through-retrieval verification, and backup restoration is demonstrated. Any failed gate keeps writes closed; after the first V2 authority mutation the response is forward repair rather than V1 rollback.
- "Explain an invalid metadata workbook" could mean returning one generic safe failure or exposing raw values and parser exceptions. Resolved: failed controlled validation creates a **Metadata Workbook Validation Report** with total count and at most the first one hundred sheet-, row-, and field-scoped stable codes plus localized-action keys; it never echoes cell values, rule text, storage locators, or library exceptions. Structural and value errors fail the Import operation, while valid concurrent divergence succeeds into a conflict-bearing Metadata Workbook Import Preview and is not mislabeled as file invalidity.
- "Execute a metadata workbook round trip" could mean one upload-and-overwrite endpoint or three durable intent boundaries. Resolved: Generate Workbook Export, Create Workbook Import Preview, and Apply Workbook Import Preview are separate idempotent asynchronous commands with durable operations. Export and Preview bind exact Source, revision, Profile, anchor, review, and artifact identities without mutating Source authority; Apply accepts only the stored Preview identity and reason, revalidates freshness, atomically changes Current Review Drafts, advances review generation and Source revision, invalidates prepared publication, and consumes the Preview plus Export. Download is permission-checked streaming through Proof Agent rather than an S3 URL.
- "Fit a large review into Workbook V2" could mean automatic multi-file sharding or one bounded atomic review artifact. Resolved: one Document Revision and Workbook contain at most 10,000 canonical Rule Units, a 10 MiB compressed package, 80 MiB expanded package, 32 MiB normalized payload, 4,096 characters per cell, 512 characters per safe Rule Unit preview, and 10,000 Reference Values. Exceeding review capacity blocks at Documents with `metadata_review_capacity_exceeded` and a split-or-restructure action rather than partial export.
- "Use Excel dropdowns without permitting formulas" could mean rejecting every formula-like OOXML element or allowing arbitrary spreadsheet expressions. Resolved: every cell formula is rejected, while only **Registered Workbook Validation Range** references emitted in the exact Export manifest may appear in Data Validation. Import validates their static internal range semantics rather than byte layout so supported Excel and LibreOffice rewrites remain compatible; unknown defined names, functions, dynamic references, external links, DDE, macros, ActiveX, OLE, data connections, embedded packages, and external pivot caches fail closed. Spreadsheet protection never replaces server Profile validation.
- "Import rule metadata from Excel" could mean indexing the workbook as evidence, joining rows by filename, creating a competing spreadsheet proposal, or updating the governed working draft. Resolved: **Insurance Rule Metadata Workbook Import** accepts only a server-generated versioned export, binds every change to exact review and structural identities, rejects macro execution and external links, and persists original, normalized, preview, and decision artifacts. It edits the same **Current Insurance Rule Metadata Review Draft** as Dashboard; differences from the immutable **Insurance Rule Metadata Parser Proposal** remain visible and require an audit reason but are not conflicts, while divergent concurrent edits create **Metadata Review Draft Conflict** through the three-way preview. Client-supplied anchors or parser authority facts are never accepted, and missing parser values create `needs_input` fields rather than a workbook-only review.
- "Transport and execute a metadata workbook import" could mean Base64 JSON plus synchronous parsing, direct artifact-store upload, or the same governed file-command protocol as document intake. Resolved: one `.xlsx` is streamed as multipart through Proof Agent with `document_id`, `revision_id`, `expected_revision`, and Knowledge Source Command Idempotency; the API returns a durable operation, and a Knowledge Worker validates and atomically creates the complete review set or no reviews.
- "Authorize Insurance Rule Metadata work" could mean granting one broad Source permission or separating proposal, decision, and publication authority. Resolved: `knowledge_source.edit` edits Current Insurance Rule Metadata Review Drafts, exports/imports Workbooks, resolves Import Preview conflicts, and changes Source Profile binding; `knowledge_source.review` approves or rejects exact current Review revisions; `knowledge_source.publish` prepares and publishes the Source. One actor may hold all three in a small deployment, while explicit four-eyes policy may forbid self-approval without changing command contracts.
- "Authorize Insurance Metadata Profile governance" could mean reusing Source permissions or granting distinct shared-asset authority. Resolved: `knowledge_profile.view`, `knowledge_profile.edit`, and `knowledge_profile.publish` separately govern Profile discovery, Draft Revision editing, and publication. Source editors may bind a visible published Profile Revision but cannot mutate or publish the shared Profile through Source or Workbook commands.
- "Insurance rule granularity" could mean one scope per document, splitting every table cell, or using the smallest coherent business rule. Resolved: use **Insurance Rule Unit** with **Inherited Insurance Rule Scope** so most documents carry reviewed document-level defaults while exceptional sections, clauses, table rows, or row groups receive reviewed overrides; fixed-size chunks and isolated cells are not rule authority units.
- "Approve metadata for every Rule Unit" could mean repeating identical decisions for every canonical anchor or approving a revision-bound default plus reviewed exceptions. Resolved: one **Document Metadata Default Review** is mandatory and bound to the exact document revision plus complete anchor-set identity; **Rule Unit Metadata Override Review** is required only for divergent, uncertain, conflicting, or operator-designated anchors. Publication materializes complete **Approved Insurance Rule Metadata** for every Rule Unit, and a changed revision or anchor set invalidates the prior default review.
- "Describe metadata review status" could mean mixing data completeness, correction actions, approval outcome, and candidate freshness in one state. Resolved: **Insurance Rule Metadata Review** uses only `needs_input`, `ready_for_approval`, `approved`, and `rejected` as business states; **Current Insurance Rule Metadata Review Revision** is a separate currency dimension. Correction and workbook application are **Insurance Rule Metadata Review Decision** history facts, an approved or rejected revision remains immutable, and later metadata changes create a new current review revision without rewriting the prior result.
- "Save metadata form edits" could mean autosaving every interaction or committing one explicit business change. Resolved: Dashboard keeps edits local with an unsaved-change guard until `Save Draft`; one **Metadata Review Draft Change** binds the exact Review version and identity, includes a required batch reason, records the field diff, and advances Source authority only on success. Completeness computes `needs_input` versus `ready_for_approval`; approval is disabled while local edits are unsaved, and changing approved metadata first creates a new current Review revision.
- "Approve many metadata reviews" could mean a Source-wide approve-all shortcut, repeated individual commands, or one exact selected-set decision. Resolved: the **Document Metadata Default Review** always requires separate confirmation, while explicitly selected, current `ready_for_approval` overrides from one document revision may use **Rule Unit Metadata Batch Approval**. The command binds the review-set generation and every selected review identity, fails atomically if any selection changes, and records both batch and per-review audit facts; hidden, unresolved, stale, or unselected reviews are never implied by a filter or count.
- "Change approved rule metadata without changing source text" could mean updating the existing search record or creating immutable authority history. Resolved: **Insurance Rule Unit Revision** includes canonical content lineage, Approved Insurance Rule Metadata revision, and Approved Insurance Knowledge Visibility Scope revision; any approved authority or visibility change creates a new revision and publication-membership interval while an optional logical rule key is diff-only and never runtime authority.
- "Missing institution authorization context" could mean refusing every request or allowing unrestricted tool lookup. Resolved: without sufficient **Institution Authorization Context**, the Agent may answer a **Public Insurance Knowledge Query** from approved knowledge sources, but must not use **Institution Business Read Tool** bindings for scoped reports, policy records, claim records, customer records, or agent records.
- "Institution, region, channel, and role metadata" could mean only rule applicability, only retrieval relevance, or knowledge visibility. Resolved: **Authorized Insurance Knowledge Scope** is evaluated separately from **Insurance Rule Applicability** and relevance; unauthorized documents and rule units are excluded before candidate generation, while audit projections do not reveal excluded source identities.
- "An empty visibility array" could mean unrestricted access, no access, or missing review. Resolved: **Approved Insurance Knowledge Visibility Scope** requires explicit `PUBLIC` or `RESTRICTED` visibility and explicit `ALL` or `ALLOWLIST` mode for every restricted dimension; missing or unapproved scope blocks publication, values are ORed within one allowlist, dimensions are ANDed, and runtime intersects the result with Institution Authorization Context.
- "Institution specialist answer shape" could mean one final answer for every audience or separate operator and external-facing views. Resolved: use **Institution Specialist Response Projection** for the staff-facing answer and include an **External Wording Draft** only when the request source or use case is customer or agent communication.
- "Institution specialist recommendation" could mean locating clauses, providing advisory synthesis, or issuing a formal underwriting or sales decision. Resolved: the highest Knowledge-backed authority is an **Evidence-Bound Specialist Recommendation** that requires staff confirmation; it may not become formal business authorization through model wording or prompt configuration.
- "Balanced low-confidence guidance" could mean warning on any uncertainty or distinguishing retrieval uncertainty from business authority. Resolved: **Insurance Rule Authority Gate** is a non-negotiable prerequisite; only after it passes may an **Advisory Evidence Warning** expose bounded completeness or interpretation uncertainty while still producing a staff-confirmed recommendation.
- "Institution specialist intent routing" could mean a fixed business taxonomy only, unrestricted LLM-defined execution, or dynamic business planning inside a governed workflow. Resolved: use **Insurance Specialist Intent Taxonomy** as a baseline anchor, allow the **LLM ReAct Planner** to create a **Dynamic Insurance Business Subplan** for any insurance-related intent, and keep executable actions, tool scope, Workflow Template topology, and policy authority under the Control Envelope.
- "Insurance Knowledge questions" could mean mostly direct clause lookup or a mixed production workload. Resolved: the current planning profile is approximately 30 percent **Insurance Clause Lookup Query**, 50 percent **Insurance Conditional Guidance Query**, and 20 percent **Insurance Rule Comparison Query**; this is a measurable evaluation weighting, not a permanent product invariant.
- "Comparison retrieval completeness" could mean accepting the globally highest-ranked passages or requiring evidence for every comparison subject. Resolved: create one or more **Comparison Required Evidence Slot** values for each product, version, scope, or requested dimension; missing required slots cause clarification or no-comparison behavior instead of a one-sided answer.
- "Institution specialist memory" could mean current-case follow-up context or a long-lived business fact store. Resolved: use **Institution Specialist Case Memory** for current task focus, clarified identifiers, scoped filters, and response-format preferences only; policy status, claim status, report values, tool payloads, and customer or agent identity facts remain live business records, not memory facts.
- "Payment guarantee" could mean an ordinary unsupported question or a high-risk service commitment request. Resolved: use **Payment Or Coverage Guarantee Request** and create an internal **Customer Escalation Handoff** while returning customer-safe refusal wording.
- "Customer lookup" could mean generic retrieval, authenticated account lookup, or a transaction. Resolved: use **Customer-Specific Read Tool** for authenticated read-only account facts.
- "Customer status lookup" could mean policy status, claim status, or a generic customer profile dump. Resolved: V1 exposes **Policy Status Lookup Tool** and **Claim Status Lookup Tool** only.
- "Missing claim id" could mean asking about all claims, guessing the newest claim, or asking the customer to clarify. Resolved: when multiple customer-owned claims match and no unique claim is resolved, V1 asks through a **Customer Resource Disambiguation Prompt** and does not propose a concrete lookup until a follow-up resolves exactly one resource.
- "The first one" could mean ordinary language understanding or unsafe default selection. - "List all my claims" could mean a safe disambiguation aid or a bulk customer resource listing. Resolved: V1 read tools support **Single-Resource Customer Read** only; bounded disambiguation handles are allowed, but bulk status listing is future capability work.
- "Customer planner tool scope" could mean a generic customer lookup tool or the concrete V1 status lookup tools. Resolved: customer-mode **Tool Proposal Scope** includes **Policy Status Lookup Tool** and **Claim Status Lookup Tool** when declared by the Agent Contract and Tool Contracts; proposals remain non-executable until authorized.
- "Approval for customer lookup" could mean human approval or policy authorization. Resolved: V1 customer-facing read lookups are **Policy-Authorized Read Tool** calls; human approval is reserved for assisted or higher-risk flows.
- "Read-tool authorization failure" could mean exposing access-control details to the customer or recording an internal denial. Resolved: V1 uses **Customer Tool Authorization Denial** internally and returns only customer-safe refusal or clarification text externally.
- "Tool Gateway failure" could mean authorization denial, insufficient evidence, or runtime failure.
